The Pricing Rule (kill the .99)

This convention is mandatory and right now it's not being followed. Fix it on every single PPV.

The one rule

Stop sending prices that end in .99. A $49.99 PPV feels like an online checkout — it screams "store." Send a clean $43 instead. Same content, way easier yes.

✕ What you're doing
$49.99
Reads as "$50." Brain flips into serious-purchase mode → he pauses, thinks, hesitates.
→
✓ What to do
$43
Reads as noticeably cheaper, still premium, casual enough to impulse-buy. Only ~$7 less.

Why this actually works

It's not about the $7. It's about which "mode" his brain switches into the second he sees the number.

🧠

The "50" trap

Once a fan sees 50, he mentally files it as a big spend and starts justifying it. $43 never triggers that.

⚡

Impulse vs decision

$43 feels like a tap. $49.99 feels like a checkout. We want a tap, not a decision.

💎

Still premium

Clean odd numbers ($43, $69, $99) feel confident and intentional — not discounted or cheap.

How to Actually Sell

Pricing only matters if the fan wants it. This is the part that makes the price feel cheap.

Slow down — connection first

Firing PPVs the second a fan says hi is the fastest way to a no sale (or a sale far below what it could've been). There are millions of OF models and endless free nudes online. He's not paying for pixels — he's paying for the girlfriend experience. Your job is to make him feel why your creator is special and why his money belongs with her. Build that first, then sell.

The flow on every chat

Open with a free tease

Always a free teasing pic or a teasing image from the script. Earn the convo.

Build the connection (GFE)

Make him feel seen and special. No connection = no maxed sale. This is the whole game.

Find what HE wants

Upsell toward his actual desire. Never lead with the finale — escalate to it.

Describe the PPV when you send it

Always sell what's inside — in a sexy way. He needs to know exactly what he's unlocking before he pays.

Follow up after every PPV

Send a short message right after. Keep momentum, keep him in the chat, set up the next sale.

🔥

Describe, don't just drop. "just sent you something 🙈" closes way less than a sentence that makes him picture it. Sell the PPV in the message that delivers it.

5

Fan Types & How to Sell Each

Not every fan gets the same play. Figure out which of the four he is, then sell him the right way.

70%

Freebie hunters

~0% of revenue

Came for free content, scroll your whole wall before they DM, looking for a handout. Don't sink time here. Test them with a $3–$5 micro PPV framed as the deal of their life. If they bite, their psychology shifts toward buying and you can move them up. If they don't, drop them and move on.

20%

Casual buyers

steady mid revenue

Will buy a PPV, chat a bit, spend anywhere from $50 to thousands over time. They want good content at the right price, not deep connection. Send PPVs 2–3x a week with strong framing, rotate content types so it stays fresh, never spam to the point they feel pressured. Space it out, keep them buying.

8%

Custom buyers

high revenue

Want something exclusive and personal — a real parasocial bond. These finish full scripts and ask for customs. Build the relationship, offer customs fast and personal ("I was thinking I could make something just for you…"). Custom requests go to your Team Leader to price and arrange — never quote or promise it yourself.

2%

Whales

up to 30% of all revenue 🐋

Spot them: buy your PPV within 24h, tip without being asked, send long personal messages, ask how your day was and expect you to ask back. Handle them: reply fastest, only your best chatter touches them (tag the very top spenders so randoms don't), use his name, make him feel heard, send free content occasionally so it never feels like you're only there for money. They're fragile — if they feel ignored or used, they leave. Never ghost a whale.

Framing & objections (works on everyone)

  • Justify the price: "filmed something I've never done before, kinda nervous about it…" or "this took forever to make but I wanted it for you." Curiosity + effort makes the price feel earned.
  • Make him feel special: "I don't talk to anyone the way I talk to you" — connection is what separates you from free nudes.
  • Price objection: "aw sorry, my stuff's a little pricey, I don't wanna hurt your pockets 🙈" — never get defensive, never apologise for the value.
  • Give a choice, don't push: pressure kills the sale; curiosity and FOMO close it.

Building Real Connection

The chat can't feel hollow. If he feels used, he leaves. If he feels good talking to you, he stays — and spends.

Engage with everything he brings up

When a fan mentions anything — a band, a hobby, his job, his team, a show — that's an opening, not noise. Always engage with it for real. A girl who only steers back to selling feels like a vending machine. A girl who actually reacts to his world feels like a girlfriend.

If you don't know the thing he mentioned, take 30 seconds and look it up (YouTube, a quick search) so you can react genuinely. Have a real opinion. Pick favourites. Make him feel like he's talking to a person who's into him.

Reading the Fan

Two things to figure out fast: what he's really here for, and how much he can spend. Both decide how you sell.

What need is he actually filling?

Nobody pays for pixels. He's on here because something's missing in real life. Spot which of these six it is and lean the chat into it. Big spenders are usually filling three or more at once.

🛡️

Certainty

Wants someone reliable to talk to. Often going through a breakup or divorce. Be consistent, be there.

🎲

Variety

Bored, maybe married. Keep it fresh, switch up fantasies and content, never routine.

👑

Significance

Nobody makes him feel special. Make him feel desired, important, VIP.

💞

Connection

Struggles to connect with women IRL. Give the real back-and-forth bond.

📈

Growth

Wants to feel the relationship is building. Reference past chats, move it forward.

🤝

Contribution

Wants to feel he's helping / providing. Let him feel like he matters to you.

Income profiling — price the person, not just the country

T1/T2 sets the floor. Inside the tier, read the individual. $200 a night is huge for one fan and pocket change for another. Gauge it subtly — never ask "how much do you make."

  • "What's your dream vacation spot?" "Maldives, never been / saving up" → lower budget. "Eh, I can pretty much go where I want" → real money.
  • "What do you do for work?" Bus driver vs investment banker tells you how hard to price.
  • High earner → push premium content and exclusive packages. Casual → smaller, consistent buys ($10–$50 a day) that add up.
🔎

The more you know, the more he spends. Profiling is what turns the pricelist from a guess into the right number for that exact fan. Log what you learn (next section).

Fan Notes — Read & Write, Every Time

This is crucial. Repeating something he already told you instantly breaks the illusion that you remember him.

Before you type — READ the notes

Always open the fan's notes first. Nothing kills a GFE faster than asking a question he already answered, or forgetting his name. Reading first means you pick up exactly where the relationship left off.

The AI Accusation

Highest-risk situation on the platform. Handle it wrong and you can lose the fan and the account.

🚨

If a fan asks or accuses "are you AI / a bot?" — STOP

This is very dangerous. Do not improvise, do not panic-type, do not argue. The moment a fan questions or accuses the chat of being AI:

✕Consult your Team Leader immediately to diffuse the situation — this is the first move, every time.
✕Don't keep replying on your own hoping it blows over.
✕Don't get defensive or over-explain — that makes it worse.
✕Don't go silent and ghost him either.

Flag it to your Team Leader the second it happens and follow their lead on how to respond. They'll help you diffuse it cleanly. When in doubt, escalate — don't wing it.

Vault & Custom Content

Two hard rules. Breaking either one can cost the account or the creator's trust.

🚫

Never upload anything to the vault

Nothing should ever be uploaded to the vault. Not pics, not videos, not "just one quick file." This is non-negotiable. If you think something needs to go in, it doesn't, leave it alone and ask.

✕Do not upload content to the vault for any reason.
✕Do not move, re-organise, or "tidy" vault files.
🎬

Custom content requests → Team Leader

If a fan asks for any custom content, do not promise, price, or arrange it yourself. Consult your Team Leader to get it made. They handle whether it's possible, the timeline, and the price.

Your job is to capture exactly what the fan wants and pass it up. Never commit the creator to something that hasn't been cleared.
