A fan returns to ask about an offer discussed yesterday. The new chatter can see the conversation, but cannot find the agreed price, the available content or who can approve an exception. The account has coverage. The handover is missing.

Before outsourcing Fanvue chatting for an AI model, put the decisions people need in one shared brief. This guide gives you a starting structure, a fill-in template and an example of a useful shift note. It is intended for operators with an active persona, approved content and an existing source of fans.

01 / Start with the account

What is the team taking over?

Describe the problem in your current inbox. Are replies waiting overnight? Do different shifts repeat offers? Are you spending your day supervising chatters? Those are specific issues a managed team can discuss with you.

If few fans are arriving, record that too. Chatting coverage does not replace audience acquisition. Keep traffic generation, content production and inbox management as separate responsibilities, even when one person currently does all three.

02 / One reference for every shift

Write the decisions down once

A persona brief should help a chatter answer a real question. A long character biography is less useful if it leaves the offer, tone or approval process unclear. Start with these six fields and give the document an owner and a version date.

  1. Persona and disclosure

    Record the approved description of the AI-generated persona, consistent fictional details, and the disclosure wording that must remain clear. Do not ask chatters to contradict that disclosure.

  2. Voice examples

    Provide a few short examples of the tone you want, plus wording to avoid. Explain whether replies are concise, playful or conversational. Examples are a reference for judgment, not a script to paste into every conversation.

  3. Content and offers

    Identify available content, approved prices and any permitted discount range. Include delivery timing and who approves custom requests. Remove expired offers from the current brief.

  4. Boundaries and escalation

    List promises the team cannot make and questions it must pass to you. Name a decision-maker and an agreed way to reach them. An unanswered escalation should stay visible to the next shift.

  5. Existing fan context

    Keep relevant purchases, unresolved issues and agreed follow-ups in your approved account workspace. Record what the fan actually said; avoid guesses about personal circumstances. A general persona brief does not need copies of private conversations.

  6. Coverage and ownership

    Set the changeover time, the person responsible for each shift and how notes are updated. During a switch between teams, agree when the outgoing team stops replying.

03 / Set up the working arrangement

Check access before the first shift

Fanvue provides manager accounts and team-member invitations for delegated work. Its manager-account guidance says managers cannot access payouts, change the creator's account email or password, or delete the account.

The same guidance says team members can access all creators under a manager account by default. Check that scope before inviting a chatter; confirm which accounts each person should be able to see and how their access will be removed at the end of the arrangement. Use the current platform instructions when setting up invitations.

For AI-generated media, keep the required disclosure in place. Fanvue's AI-content guidance requires a clear, prominent disclosure. Hiring human chatters does not change how the persona or media was created.

Platform references checked October 3, 2026. Recheck the linked instructions during onboarding; permissions and policies can change.

04 / Make continuity practical

Leave the next person a decision they can act on

A useful shift note says what happened, what remains open and what to do next. A label such as “interested fan” leaves the incoming chatter to reconstruct the conversation.

Example shift note · illustrative

Context: Fan asked about the Friday release and has already seen the standard offer.
Open item: Their additional request needs the operator's approval.
Next action: Check the approval thread before replying. Keep the agreed Friday follow-up; do not promise an unapproved delivery.

Keep the detailed note beside the conversation in the workspace your team uses. The shared persona brief should explain the note format and approval route so every shift follows the same process.

05 / Review the work you agreed

Look beyond the revenue total

Record a starting picture before the handover: recent traffic, new fans, messaging coverage and the revenue categories available in your account. Then review the first week against the problem you set out to solve.

Three questions for the first review
QuestionEvidence to examine
Did coverage improve?Unanswered conversations, agreed hours and missed follow-ups.
Did the team follow the brief?A sample of conversations, offer accuracy and resolved escalations.
What changed financially?Tips and PPV after relevant fees, alongside traffic and new-fan changes.

Write down changes in traffic, content releases or pricing. A higher or lower total does not isolate the effect of chatting when those inputs also changed. Ask the team what it will adjust next and when you will review that adjustment.

06 / Before you start

Questions about outsourcing Fanvue chatting

Can I hand over an AI-model account with very little traffic?

You can prepare the brief now. Before hiring coverage, check whether enough fans arrive to create a messaging workload. If acquisition is the main problem, adding chatters alone will not solve it.

Do I need to give a chatting agency my password for an estimate?

No account access is needed for Zinely's initial estimate. Describe your platform, current numbers and what you want to improve. Agree the service and the appropriate platform access before chatting begins.

What if I am replacing an existing team?

Agree a changeover time, preserve useful notes in your approved workspace and identify unresolved promises or requests. Confirm who is replying during the transition and remove access that is no longer needed.
