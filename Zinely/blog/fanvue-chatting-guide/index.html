A new subscriber joins at 11 p.m. your time. He gets your automated welcome, writes back “hey, love the pics,” and waits. Nobody answers until lunchtime the next day. By then he's spent his evening, and his money, somewhere else.

Fanvue chatting is the work of turning the fans you already have into conversations, and those conversations into tips, paid messages and customs. The subscription gets a fan through the door. A lot of what he spends after that happens in the DMs. Good chatting is easy to describe and hard to do every day: answer while he's still there, remember who he is, offer something when he's interested and not before, and come back when you said you would.

This is the hub of our Fanvue series. It covers how the DMs earn and what good chatting looks like in practice. The other guides go deeper on pricing PPV, giving a chatter access safely, what chatters cost and hiring chatters yourself.

01 / The money

Where Fanvue DM money comes from

Four things pay on a Fanvue account: subscriptions, tips, paid messages (media with a price on it, sent in the chat) and custom requests. Chatting doesn't set your subscription price. It drives the other three.

Fanvue keeps its platform fee first. Its creator earnings policy sets the standard creator earning rate at 80% of gross revenue from paid services, with 20% kept as platform fees. Promotional rates can apply on top of that, so check your own earnings page. When anyone quotes you a percentage for chatting, ask whether it's taken before or after that fee.

Most fans will never spend like that. But a fan who does is built over months of conversations that remembered what he liked, not by one clever message.

02 / First contact

The welcome message and the first real reply

Fanvue lets you set automated messages that go out to new followers and new subscribers, with a different message for each group. You'll find them under Settings, Creator settings, Automated Messages. Use them. Just don't treat the welcome as the sale.

A good welcome is short, sounds like the creator or persona, and asks something easy to answer. The answer tells your chatter something useful.

Weak

“Hey babe, thanks for subbing. Check out my new set, only $25, you won't regret it.”

It's a price tag before a hello. A new fan has no reason to pay yet, and a reply to this is awkward to write.

Better

“Hey, glad you found me. Quick one so I know when to catch you: where in the world are you?”

Easy to answer, and the answer gives you his time zone. That decides when your follow-ups land.

The first human reply matters more than the automated one. If he answers and hears nothing for hours, the welcome did its job and you threw it away.

03 / Rapport

Build rapport you can actually use later

Rapport isn't small talk for its own sake. It's collecting the details that make the next conversation easier and the next offer feel personal. A fan who says he finishes work at six on Fridays and gets paid that day has just told you when to message him.

Write those details down where the next shift will see them:

  • Name or nickname he likes, city and time zone
  • What he's bought, and what he turned down
  • What he likes, and anything he said he doesn't want
  • Promises made to him (“I'll send you the rest on Friday”)
  • Payday or other timing he's mentioned

On AI-model accounts, the persona has to hold together across every chatter and every shift: same city, same backstory, same way of talking. Fanvue's AI content guidance also requires clear, prominent disclosure of AI media, and says a fully AI account must never tell fans it's a real person. Your chatters need to know that line before their first shift. Our Fanvue persona brief has a template for writing all of this down once.

04 / Selling

How to offer PPV without it feeling like a pitch

The best moment for a paid message is when the fan has just shown interest: he compliments a photo, asks what else you've got, or comes back when he said he would. An offer that answers him lands very differently from one dropped into a cold “hi.”

Example exchange · illustrative

Fan: that red set was something else
Chatter: You've got good taste. I shot a few more in it that never went on my page. Want them?
Fan: obviously
Chatter: [paid message with a blurred preview] Just for you. Tell me which one's your favourite.

The offer follows his lead, the caption hints without overselling (Fanvue's PPV guide says captions should only describe what's actually included), and the last line keeps the chat going after he pays.

If he says no, don't jump to half price. Keep talking, or come back to it another day. The price should come from a written ladder, not from whoever's on shift. Our Fanvue PPV pricing guide covers how to set one.

05 / Keeping fans

Follow-ups, big spenders and time zones

Follow-ups. Every “maybe after payday” is a date in the diary. A fan who hears back exactly when he said he'd be ready feels remembered. A fan who's promised something on Friday and gets nothing stops believing the next promise.

Big spenders. Your top fans should get conversations, not the same mass messages as everyone else. Check what they've already bought before any offer. One repeated PPV can undo months of goodwill with someone who's paying for the personal touch.

Time zones. Look at when your fans actually write. If a lot of them are in North America and you're in Europe, their evening is your middle of the night. Either somebody covers those hours or those fans learn that nobody answers. That's usually when creators start looking at a Fanvue chatting agency or hiring their own team.

08 / Before you go

Common questions

Can I use an AI chatbot for Fanvue chatting?

Some operators do. We think a fan spending real money should get a person who has read his history and can make a judgement call. At Zinely, human chatters send every message, and AI is used only inside our own tools to give chatters each fan's context. Our comparison of human chatters and AI chatbots goes through the trade-offs.

How fast should I reply to Fanvue messages?

While the fan is still around, ideally. A reply within minutes catches him in the same mood he wrote in. A reply the next afternoon often reaches someone who has moved on. If you can't cover the hours your fans are awake, that's the gap to fix first.

No. On Fanvue, commission is usually 20–25% of the net tips and PPV our chatters close, with final terms agreed before launch. Subscriptions and renewals aren't commissioned, and Fanvue's platform fee still applies.
