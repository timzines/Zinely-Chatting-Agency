Fanvue chatter cost: hourly, commission or doing it yourself.

Two quotes arrive on the same day. One chatter wants a flat hourly rate plus a small cut of sales. An agency wants a percentage and nothing else. They look impossible to compare, because one costs money every hour and the other only costs money when something sells.

Fanvue chatters are paid in one of three ways: you do it yourself and pay with your time, you hire freelancers by the hour (often with a commission on top), or you use a commission-only agency that takes a percentage of the sales its chatters close. Which is cheapest depends on how much your DMs sell and how many hours need covering. There's no honest single number for “what chatters cost,” so this guide shows how each model behaves, with a worked example you can redo.

01 / Before anyone else

Start with what Fanvue takes

Fanvue's creator earnings policy sets the standard creator earning rate at 80% of gross revenue from paid services. The other 20% is Fanvue's platform fee. The same policy says promotional earning rates can apply automatically when they're running, so your own earnings page is the final word for your account.

At the standard rate, a $100 tip puts $80 in your Fanvue balance. That gap matters as soon as someone quotes you a chatting commission, because 25% of $100 and 25% of $80 aren't the same bill. Always ask which one they mean.

Doing it yourself is the cheapest on paper and the most expensive in time. It's fine early on. It breaks down when fans write at hours you're asleep, or when chatting eats the time you'd spend on content and promotion.

Hourly freelancers give you control and a predictable bill. The catch is that you pay for presence, not results, and you become the manager. Covering every hour of a 30-day month is about 720 chatter-hours, which means several people, a rota and someone to call when the 3 a.m. shift doesn't show. Our guide to hiring Fanvue chatters covers that side.

A commission-only agency with no minimum costs nothing in a week where its chatters close nothing, which puts the incentive in the right place. You're trusting them with your fans, though, so the questions in section four matter more than the headline rate.

04 / Before you sign

Questions to ask any chatter or agency about cost

  1. Is the percentage taken from gross or net?

    Gross is before Fanvue's fee, net is after. On the same sales, a gross-based 25% is a bigger bill.

  2. Which revenue is included?

    Only the tips and PPV their chatters close, or everything on the account, subscriptions and renewals included?

  3. How do they know which sales they closed?

    Ask how sales are attributed and how you can check it yourself.

  4. Is there a retainer, minimum or setup fee?

    A minimum turns commission pricing back into a fixed cost.

  5. How long are you locked in?

    Notice periods and exit terms matter more than a percentage point.

  6. Who pays and covers the chatters?

    If someone misses a shift, whose problem is it?

  7. Can you change prices and offers?

    You should approve the price ladder. See our Fanvue PPV pricing guide.

06 / Before you go

Common questions

Is a commission-only chatting agency always cheaper?

No. In quiet months it usually costs less than paying chatters by the hour, because the bill falls with sales. In very busy months an hourly team can cost less on paper, before you count the time you spend hiring, scheduling and reviewing.

How much does Fanvue take from creators?

Fanvue's creator earnings policy sets the standard creator earning rate at 80% of gross revenue from paid services, with 20% kept as platform fees. Promotional rates can apply, so check your own earnings page.

Does Zinely charge commission on Fanvue subscriptions?

No. Commission is usually 20–25% of the net tips and PPV our chatters close, with final terms agreed before launch. Subscriptions and renewals aren't commissioned.
